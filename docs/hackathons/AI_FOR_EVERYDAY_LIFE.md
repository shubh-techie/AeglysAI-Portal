# Challenge 06 — AI for Everyday Life

Portal-defined extension authorized by the user on 2026-10-11. Beginner-to-intermediate difficulty. The research COM-003A offering still defines five tracks; this sixth track is not claimed to be part of that upstream contract or published research work.

Build a practical, intelligent solution to a meaningful everyday human problem using AI, ML, Generative AI or intelligent automation. Participants may propose any appropriate real-world problem and explain its intended users.

Example areas: education and learning; accessibility; personal productivity; sustainability and food waste; community assistance; general wellness; financial literacy; scam awareness. These are idea areas, not medical, legal or financial decision services or validated outcomes.

## Minimum prototype and evidence

- Any programming language or framework; working MVP, functional prototype or proof of concept with meaningful intelligent functionality.
- Clear problem statement and intended users; demonstrable output and basic tests or evaluation evidence.
- Privacy-safe synthetic or authorized inputs with data/model attribution, licensing and limitations.
- No unsupported medical, legal or financial decision claims. Keep personal data, confidential/patent material and credentials out of inputs, recordings, public Git and AI prompts.
- Public source, README, reproducibility/setup instructions and required maximum-two-minute working demonstration video. Narrated or silent captioned recordings accepted; webcam not required.

Suggested README sections: problem; intended users; selected challenge AI for Everyday Life (ai-for-everyday-life); implemented AI/ML/GenAI/automation behavior; safe input provenance; setup/run/test commands; demonstrable output; evaluation; attribution/licenses; limitations. This is a submission outline, not a sample participant record or asserted result.

Every member must complete mandatory individual AeglysAI registration, fork/clone/run A/B/C baseline, architecture/setup/observation/improvement/validation evidence and selection before building. Model D knowledge unnecessary. Individuals or selected teams of two to four, maximum four; existing leader/team-ID/private roster rules apply unchanged. Standalone final implementations accepted after qualification.

Both agreed 100-point rubrics remain unchanged: Round 1 screens source/prototype/video/reproducibility and selects finalists; Round 2 five-minute live demonstration plus five-minute Q&A ranks finalists.
